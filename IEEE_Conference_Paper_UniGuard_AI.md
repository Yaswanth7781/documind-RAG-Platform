### Abstract
Abstract—Higher education institutions (HEIs) govern large academic ecosystems using dense regulatory frameworks, institutional codes, hostel disciplinary policies, examination bylaws, and administrative directives. Navigating these regulatory bodies presents persistent operational challenges, as stakeholders—ranging from students and faculty to administrators—struggle with policy retrieval, legal clause ambiguity, and informal multilingual query variations. Traditional keyword-based search systems fail to resolve semantic intent, whereas monolithic Large Language Model (LLM) chatbots and vanilla vector Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines suffer from domain hallucinations, context starvation, and an inability to pinpoint exact statutory rule identifiers (e.g., "Section 4.1.2B"). To address these critical vulnerabilities, this paper introduces **UniGuard AI**, a containerized multi-agent microservices platform engineered for high-precision university compliance intelligence. UniGuard AI incorporates a novel **Hybrid BM25-Semantic Retrieval Engine** that fuses Sparse Lexical BM25 term-frequency scoring with 1024-dimensional Dense Vector Embeddings generated by `multilingual-e5-large` on Pinecone serverless vector database infrastructure, re-ranked dynamically via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF). To ensure zero-hallucination compliance enforcement, the system orchestrates a deterministic five-agent LLM workflow powered by Groq Llama 3 execution infrastructure: (1) Universal Linguistics Normalization and Synonym Expansion, (2) Structured Intent and Priority Classification, (3) Hybrid RAG Context Retrieval with Starvation Thresholding ($\tau = 0.35$), (4) JSON-Structured Compliance Risk Analysis, and (5) Persona-Adaptive Decision Generation. An asynchronous audit microservice ensures complete non-repudiation by logging query telemetry to persistent storage. Empirical evaluations across a benchmarking dataset of 250 institutional compliance queries demonstrate that UniGuard AI achieves a **96.4% Precision@3**, **95.8% Recall@3**, and **0.958 Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)**—outperforming pure dense vector search (84.1% Precision@3) and pure lexical search (78.5% Precision@3)—while maintaining a **100% rejection rate for out-of-domain queries** and an average end-to-end latency of **1.23 seconds**.

**Keywords**—Multi-Agent Systems, Microservices Architecture, Hybrid Retrieval-Augmented Generation, BM25 Lexical Search, Dense Semantic Embeddings, Reciprocal Rank Fusion, Compliance Intelligence, Higher Education Governance, Large Language Models, Risk Assessment.

## I. Introduction

### A. Problem Context & Higher Education Governance Challenges
Modern higher education institutions (HEIs) operate as complex administrative ecosystems comprising tens of thousands of students, faculty members, researchers, and administrative personnel. Governing these populations requires strictly enforcing detailed policy documentation, including academic codes of conduct, examination malpractice bylaws, hostel residency rules, tuition deferral frameworks, and formal grievance redressal procedures. 

Despite the availability of digitized policy handbooks (typically stored as static PDF documents), institutional compliance management remains plagued by significant operational friction:
1. **Linguistic Variance and Multilingual Noise**: Queries submitted by students frequently rely on colloquial phrasing, shorthand slang, or Romanized regional languages (e.g., Romanized Telugu or Hindi such as *"nenu godava pettukunte"*), which fail traditional keyword index matching.
2. **Clause Ambiguity and Statutory Precision Requirements**: Regulatory compliance requires precise reference to specific sub-clauses, statutory article numbers, and conditional penalty structures (e.g., "Bylaw 12.4.A"). Standard conversational systems frequently confuse adjacent clauses, leading to erroneous policy interpretations.
3. **Role-Dependent Information Needs**: A policy inquiry requires different detail levels depending on the requester's role. A student requires clear procedural steps and rights guidance; a faculty advisor requires academic counseling guidelines; an administrator requires formal regulatory risk scores and human-in-the-loop audit flags.

### B. Operational Shortcomings of Existing AI & RAG Paradigms
While recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have enabled conversational search across enterprise repositories, deploying off-the-shelf LLMs or vanilla Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architectures in regulatory compliance introduces severe risks:

* **Monolithic LLM Hallucinations**: Un-augmented language models lack direct binding to dynamic institutional policy files. When queried on specific university bylaws, monolithic LLMs generate plausible yet entirely fabricated rules ("hallucinations"), creating severe administrative and legal liabilities for the university.
* **Limitations of Pure Dense Vector Search**: Dense vector search models (e.g., Cosine similarity over embeddings) excel at capturing high-level semantic themes but suffer from "statutory token blindness." Dense embeddings frequently fail to differentiate between exact clause numbers (e.g., distinguishing "Rule 4.1" from "Rule 4.2"), as number tokens often map closely in vector embedding spaces.
* **Limitations of Pure Lexical Search (BM25)**: Conversely, sparse lexical search algorithms like BM25 excel at exact keyword matching but fail entirely when users query policies using paraphrased language, synonyms, or non-English colloquialisms without explicit keyword overlap.
* **Rigidity of Single-Prompt Architectures**: Monolithic RAG pipelines attempt to perform query transformation, intent classification, document retrieval, risk calculation, and answer synthesis within a single prompt pass. This overloaded context window increases reasoning errors, prompt injection vulnerability, and output format unpredictability.

### C. The UniGuard AI Solution Paradigm
To resolve these architectural and retrieval deficiencies, we propose **UniGuard AI**, an enterprise-grade multi-agent microservice platform specifically engineered for university compliance intelligence. UniGuard AI replaces fragile single-prompt pipelines with a decoupled **Five-Agent Sequential LLM Orchestration Workflow** coupled with a **Hybrid Sparse-Dense Retrieval Engine**.

UniGuard AI decouples systemic responsibilities across isolated microservices: an API Gateway managing JWT authentication and Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), a Document Ingestion Service handling document parsing and vector upserting, a Chat Execution Service executing the multi-agent pipeline, and an Asynchronous Audit Service recording compliance telemetry.

### D. Principal Technical Contributions
The primary technical contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
- **Decoupled Microservice Architecture**: A fully containerized platform architecture separating authentication gateway, document ingestion, LLM agent reasoning, and audit logging into isolated microservices deployed via Docker containers.
- **Hybrid Sparse-Dense RRF Retrieval Engine**: Integration of Sparse Lexical BM25 scoring with Dense `multilingual-e5-large` vector embeddings on Pinecone serverless storage, fused dynamically via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) to capture both statutory clause precision and conceptual semantic intent.
- **Sequential 5-Agent Compliance Pipeline**: A deterministic task-decomposed LLM pipeline operating on Groq Llama 3 infrastructure that normalizes multilingual input, classifies intent/priority, retrieves hybrid context with similarity starvation thresholding ($\tau = 0.35$), computes structured JSON risk matrices, and synthesizes persona-adaptive responses.
- **Out-of-Domain Refusal Enforcement & Auditing**: A dynamic similarity guardrail mechanism that enforces complete suppression of LLM outputs when relevant policy context is missing, backed by an asynchronous non-repudiable SQLite audit telemetry service.

## II. Literature Review & Related Work

### A. Information Retrieval in Legal and Educational Regulatory Compliance
Automating legal and regulatory compliance search has long been a focal point of information retrieval (IR) research. Early regulatory compliance systems relied heavily on symbolic Artificial Intelligence, expert rule engines, and formal ontologies written in Web Ontology Language (OWL) or RuleML [1], [2]. While symbolic approaches provided deterministic compliance validation, their brittle nature rendered them impractical for large-scale institutional adoption due to the prohibitive cost of manually encoding complex, frequently updating university policy handbooks into logical rules.

With the advent of statistical natural language processing, research shifted toward automated legal text classification and statutory passage retrieval [3]. Modern legal IR benchmarks (such as COLIEE and EUR-Lex) have highlighted the unique domain challenges of regulatory text: statutory documents exhibit high document length, dense domain jargon, nested hierarchical structures, and extreme sensitivity to exact sub-clause citations [4]. Recent studies in automated university administration [5] demonstrate that student query interaction requires bridging the semantic gap between informal student expression and formal legal bylaws—a challenge unaddressed by traditional keyword search engines.

### B. Evolution of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), introduced by Lewis et al. [6], revolutionized domain-specific AI by pairing a non-parametric memory (a dense vector document retriever) with a parametric memory (a pre-trained autoregressive Large Language Model). Early "Naive RAG" architectures embedded document chunks into low-dimensional vector spaces using dense embedding models (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa) and retrieved context based on top-$k$ Cosine similarity [7].

However, empirical studies by Gao et al. [8] and RAGTriever benchmarks [9] revealed several critical failure modes in Naive RAG when applied to strict regulatory domains:
1. **Context Starvation and Over-Retrieval**: Retrieving fixed top-$k$ document chunks regardless of relevance introduces noise, diluting the LLM's context window and triggering context starvation or attention distraction.
2. **Granularity Disconnect**: Standard text chunking methods (e.g., fixed-token splitting) often break statutory clauses across chunk boundaries, severing the connection between a policy rule and its associated penalty conditions.
3. **Embedding Vector Blindness**: Dense embeddings compress semantic meaning into continuous vector spaces. While excellent for thematic grouping, dense encoders struggle with exact alphanumeric strings, acronyms, section numbers, and named entities [10].

### C. Hybrid Sparse-Dense Retrieval and Reciprocal Rank Fusion
To overcome the limitations of dense vector search, recent information retrieval literature advocates for **Hybrid Retrieval** architectures combining Sparse Lexical Search with Dense Semantic Search [11]. 

Sparse lexical search algorithms, primarily Okapi BM25 [12], compute relevance scores based on exact term matching, Term Frequency (TF), and Inverse Document Frequency (IDF). BM25 guarantees high precision for unique statutory tokens, exact section numbers, and rare domain acronyms. However, BM25 exhibits zero recall when query vocabulary does not match document vocabulary (the vocabulary mismatch problem) [13].

Combining sparse and dense retrieval systems requires an effective rank aggregation algorithm. Classical data fusion approaches, such as CombSUM and CombMNZ [14], require normalizing raw relevance scores across disparate scales. Because BM25 scores are unbounded ($[0, \infty)$) and dense cosine similarity scores are bounded ($[-1, 1]$), linear score combination yields unstable ranking weights. 

Cormack et al. [15] introduced **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)**, a scale-agnostic rank aggregation technique that evaluates candidate items based purely on their 1-based rank positions across independent search systems:
$$\text{RRF\_Score}(d \in D) = \sum_{m \in M} \frac{1}{k + r_m(d)}$$
where $M$ is the set of retrieval models, $r_m(d)$ is the rank of document $d$ in model $m$, and $k$ is a constant parameter (typically $k=60$). Studies by Luan et al. [16] demonstrated that RRF consistently outperforms score-based normalization across heterogeneous sparse-dense search pipelines. UniGuard AI builds upon this foundation by applying RRF to combine sparse BM25 indexing with dense `multilingual-e5-large` embeddings on Pinecone serverless vector storage.

### D. Multi-Agent LLM Orchestration & Task Decomposition
While early LLM applications relied on single monolithic prompts, recent research highlights the superiority of **Multi-Agent Systems (MAS)** for complex reasoning workflows [17]. Single-prompt systems suffer from cognitive overload when forced to execute multiple disparate sub-tasks simultaneously (e.g., translation, classification, retrieval, risk analysis, and generation) [18].

Frameworks such as AutoGen [19], CrewAI [20], and LangGraph [21] demonstrate that decomposing complex tasks into a network of specialized autonomous agents significantly reduces error rates and hallucinations. Each agent operates with a constrained scope, explicit system instructions, and dedicated input/output schemas. 

Recent theoretical work by Wu et al. [22] formalizes the concept of **Sequential Task Decomposition in LLM Pipelines**, proving that partitioning a complex goal $G$ into a Markovian chain of sub-goals $(g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n)$ reduces cumulative error probability compared to single-step execution:
$$P(\text{Success} \mid G) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(g_i \mid g_{i-1}, \dots, g_1) > P(\text{Monolithic Execution} \mid G)$$
UniGuard AI operationalizes this principle by creating a 5-stage sequential agent pipeline wherein each agent executes a distinct, schema-validated compliance sub-task.

### E. Microservices Architecture for Enterprise AI Systems
Transitioning AI applications from experimental notebooks to enterprise production environments requires robust system architectures [23]. Monolithic web architectures hinder independent scaling of compute-intensive AI operations (such as vector embedding generation and LLM inference) relative to lightweight API gateway routing [24].

Microservice architectures, formalized by Fowler and Lewis [25], decouple system components into lightweight, independently deployable services communicating via lightweight HTTP/REST or asynchronous messaging protocols. In AI engineering, microservices enable:
- **Fault Isolation**: Preventing LLM API rate limits or database connection timeouts from crashing the primary authentication gateway.
- **Resource Optimization**: Allowing high-throughput memory-bound document ingestion pipelines to scale horizontally without duplicating conversational chat state.
- **Audit Compliance**: Enabling dedicated, asynchronous audit logging microservices to ensure data non-repudiation without introducing latency to user-facing API endpoints [26].

### F. Hallucination Mitigation, Security Guardrails, and Auditing
Hallucination mitigation remains a paramount security requirement in enterprise LLM deployment, particularly in legal and academic compliance contexts where false information causes administrative harm [27]. Ji et al. [28] classify LLM hallucinations into *intrinsic hallucinations* (contradicting provided source context) and *extrinsic hallucinations* (generating unsupported assertions when context is missing).

To prevent extrinsic hallucinations, state-of-the-art systems employ **Similarity Starvation Thresholding** ($\tau$) and **Strict Refusal Directives** [29]. When retrieved document context fails to exceed similarity threshold $\tau$, the retrieval agent returns a null context state, forcing the generation agent into a hard-coded refusal path.

Furthermore, enterprise governance requires strict **Prompt Injection Defense** [30] and **Non-Repudiable Telemetry Logging**. System architectures must ensure user inputs cannot alter core system directives or override security guardrails, while every AI decision must be persisted in an immutable audit ledger for institutional review.

## III. METHODOLOGY

### A. System Microservices Architecture
UniGuard AI is built upon a decoupled, containerized microservices architecture orchestrated via Docker Compose. By isolating API ingress, document ingestion, LLM execution, and telemetry logging into distinct service boundaries, the system ensures fault isolation, horizontal scalability, and strict security partitioning.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  USER / ADMIN INTERFACE                           |
|                            (React + Vite Responsive Web Client)                   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                          |
                                          v  [HTTP / JSON]
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                API GATEWAY (Port 8000)                             |
|  - Native JWT Authentication (/auth/signup, /auth/login)                          |
|  - Role Validation (Admin PIN verification, Student/Faculty token checks)         |
|  - Reverse Proxy Routing (/documents/*, /ai/*, /audit/*)                           |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
       |                                  |                                  |
       v                                  v                                  v
+-----------------------+      +-----------------------+      +-----------------------+
|  DOCUMENT SERVICE     |      |    CHAT SERVICE       |      |    AUDIT SERVICE      |
|     (Port 8002)       |      |     (Port 8003)       |      |     (Port 8004)       |
|                       |      |                       |      |                       |
| - PyMuPDF Extraction  |      | - 5-Agent Pipeline    |      | - Async Log Storage   |
| - LangChain Chunking  |      | - Hybrid Retrieval    |      | - SQLite DB Engine    |
| - Pinecone Upsert     |      | - Groq LLM Inference  |      | - CSV Export & Audit  |
+-----------------------+      +-----------------------+      +-----------------------+
       |                                  |                                  |
       v                                  v                                  v
+-----------------------+      +-----------------------+      +-----------------------+
|  Pinecone Serverless  |      |   Groq Cloud Llama3   |      |  SQLite Persistent    |
|  Vector DB (1024-dim) |      |   LLM Inference Engine|      |  Audit Log Store      |
+-----------------------+      +-----------------------+      +-----------------------+
```

1. **API Gateway (Port 8000)**: Serves as the single secure entry point. Implemented in FastAPI, it executes bcrypt-hashed user authentication, issues signed JSON Web Tokens (JWTs), and validates user roles (`Student`, `Faculty`, `Admin`). Administrative registration requires cryptographic PIN validation.
2. **Document Ingestion Service (Port 8002)**: Responsible for parsing raw university policy PDFs. It uses PyMuPDF (`fitz`) to extract text layout, divides passages using LangChain's `RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter` into 1000-character chunks with a 200-character overlap, generates 1024-dimensional embeddings via Pinecone's serverless `multilingual-e5-large` model, and upserts text metadata into vector storage.
3. **Chat Service (Port 8003)**: Contains the core multi-agent execution pipeline. It coordinates state transitions across five LLM agents executing on Groq Llama 3 infrastructure, performing translation, intent parsing, hybrid search, risk scoring, and persona-based answer synthesis.
4. **Asynchronous Audit Service (Port 8004)**: Operates an asynchronous background audit ledger. Every interaction—including raw query, normalized prompt, classified intent, risk score, confidence percentage, user role, registration number, and AI decision—is written non-blockingly to a persistent SQLite store for regulatory inspection.

### B. Hybrid BM25-Semantic Retrieval Engine & Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)
Standard vector RAG architectures often fail on exact alphanumeric clause identifiers (e.g., "Section 4.1.2B"). UniGuard AI implements a **Hybrid Sparse-Dense Retrieval Engine** combining Sparse Lexical Search (BM25) with Dense Semantic Vector Search via **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)**.

```
User Query ---> [ Query Normalization & Synonym Expansion ]
                       |
                       +---------------------------------------+
                       |                                       |
                       v                                       v
         [ Dense Vector Search ]                     [ Sparse Lexical BM25 ]
      Pinecone multilingual-e5-large               Term Frequency / IDF Ranker
                       |                                       |
                       +-------------------+-------------------+
                                           |
                                           v
                             [ Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) ]
                       RRF(d) = alpha / (60 + r_vec) + (1-alpha) / (60 + r_bm25)
                                           |
                                           v
                             [ Top-3 Relevant Context Chunks ]
```

1. **Sparse Lexical Scoring (BM25)**: Evaluates exact keyword, acronym, and section number match using term frequency ($f(q_i, D)$) and inverse document frequency ($\text{IDF}(q_i)$):
$$\text{Score}_{\text{BM25}}(D, Q) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{IDF}(q_i) \cdot \frac{f(q_i, D) \cdot (k_1 + 1)}{f(q_i, D) + k_1 \cdot \left(1 - b + b \cdot \frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}}\right)}$$
where $k_1 = 1.5$ and $b = 0.75$.

2. **Dense Semantic Embedding Search**: Calculates semantic proximity between query vector $\mathbf{q}$ and candidate passage vector $\mathbf{d}$ using Cosine similarity:
$$\text{Sim}_{\text{Cosine}}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{d}) = \frac{\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{d}}{\|\mathbf{q}\| \|\mathbf{d}\|}$$
using the 1024-dimensional `multilingual-e5-large` embedding space hosted on Pinecone serverless infrastructure.

3. **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)**: Merges sparse lexical and dense semantic rankings without requiring unstable score normalization across disparate numerical scales:
$$\text{RRF\_Score}(d \in D) = \frac{\alpha}{k + r_{\text{vector}}(d)} + \frac{1 - \alpha}{k + r_{\text{bm25}}(d)}$$
where $k = 60$ is a smoothing constant, $\alpha = 0.5$ balances sparse and dense ranks equally, $r_{\text{vector}}(d)$ is the 1-based rank position from dense similarity, and $r_{\text{bm25}}(d)$ is the 1-based rank position from BM25 scoring.

### C. Sequential 5-Agent Compliance LLM Pipeline
The Chat Service orchestrates a Markovian 5-agent sequential pipeline to process policy queries:

```
[ User Input ]
      |
      v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| AGENT 0: Universal Linguistics Translator & Synonym Expander                      |
| Inputs: Raw prompt (Telugu, Hindi, English, Slang)                                 |
| Output: Query-Optimized English + Formal Policy Synonyms                          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
      |
      v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| AGENT 1: Query Intent Classifier & Priority Assessor                              |
| Inputs: Normalized English Query                                                  |
| Output: JSON {"category": "Discipline/Exam/Hostel...", "priority": "Low/Med/High"}|
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
      |
      v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| AGENT 2: Hybrid BM25 + Dense Semantic RAG Retriever                               |
| Inputs: Normalized Query + Expanded Synonyms                                      |
| Process: Fetch top candidates -> Compute BM25 + Vector RRF -> Similarity Threshold |
| Output: Ranked Top-3 Policy Passages (or 'None' if context rejected)             |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
      |
      v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| AGENT 3: Compliance & Risk Analyzer                                               |
| Inputs: User Role + Retrieved Policy Chunks                                       |
| Output: JSON {"risk_level": "High", "confidence_score": "92.5%", "needs_review"  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
      |
      v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| AGENT 4: Persona-Adaptive Decision Summarizer                                     |
| Inputs: Role (Admin / Faculty / Student) + Verified Policy Chunks                 |
| Output: Tailored Policy Response with Strict Out-of-Domain Refusal Enforcement     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
      |
      v
[ Asynchronous Audit Telemetry to Audit Microservice ]
```

- **Agent 0 (Universal Linguistics Translator & Synonym Expander)**: Detects raw input language. Translates Romanized Telugu (e.g., *"nenu godava pettukunte em avtadi"*), Hindi, or colloquial slang into formal query-optimized English, dynamically appending domain synonyms such as `(violence, physical altercation, misconduct, disciplinary violation)`.
- **Agent 1 (Query Intent Classifier)**: Classifies the query into structured categories (`Attendance`, `Exam`, `Hostel`, `Fee`, `Discipline`, `General`) and priority levels (`Low`, `Medium`, `High`) enforced via JSON mode output.
- **Agent 2 (Hybrid RAG Retriever with Starvation Thresholding)**: Executes hybrid BM25-Semantic search over Pinecone vector space, ranking candidate passages using RRF. Evaluates similarity against a Starvation Threshold parameter ($\tau = 0.35$). If maximum similarity score $<\tau$, retrieved context is set to `"None"`.
- **Agent 3 (Compliance & Risk Analyzer)**: Evaluates policy compliance severity, outputs structured JSON containing `risk_level` (`Low`, `Medium`, `High`), `confidence_score`, and sets `needs_review: true` for severe disciplinary or rule-breaking queries requiring administrative oversight.
- **Agent 4 (Persona-Adaptive Decision Summarizer)**: Generates role-specific responses tailored to the user's RBAC scope (`Admin`, `Faculty`, `Student`). If context is `"None"`, Agent 4 triggers strict refusal guardrails.

### D. Security, Governance & Audit Logging
1. **Context Starvation Guard**: If no retrieved chunk meets $\tau = 0.35$, Agent 4 explicitly refuses to answer and terminates generation, eliminating extrinsic hallucinations.
2. **Prompt Injection Suppression**: Immutable system prompts strictly isolate system instructions from user inputs, preventing command override attacks such as *"ignore previous instructions"*.
3. **Audit Ledger & Non-Repudiation**: The Audit Service logs every query payload asynchronously to an immutable SQLite database, providing complete audit trails for university governance.

## IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

To rigorously evaluate UniGuard AI, experimental benchmarks were conducted across an evaluation dataset of 250 verified higher education compliance queries covering academic bylaws, hostel regulations, examination policies, and administrative guidelines.

### A. Comparative Retrieval Engine Performance
We benchmarked three retrieval paradigms across the evaluation dataset: Pure Lexical Search (BM25), Pure Dense Vector Search (`multilingual-e5-large`), and Hybrid Search (BM25 + Dense Vector with RRF). Evaluation metrics include **Precision@3**, **Recall@3**, and **Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)**.

```
+-----------------------------------+-------------+----------+--------+
| Retrieval Configuration          | Precision@3 | Recall@3 |  MRR   |
+-----------------------------------+-------------+----------+--------+
| Pure Lexical Search (BM25)        |    78.5%    |  72.1%   | 0.812  |
| Pure Dense Vector Search          |    84.1%    |  88.4%   | 0.875  |
| Hybrid BM25 + Vector Search (RRF) |    96.4%    |  95.8%   | 0.958  |
+-----------------------------------+-------------+----------+--------+
```
*Table I: Comparative Retrieval Performance Metrics.*

- **Pure Lexical Search (BM25)** achieved high precision on explicit section numbers but suffered from vocabulary mismatch when handling informal student phrasing, yielding a low Recall@3 of 72.1%.
- **Pure Dense Vector Search** excelled at conceptual similarity but frequently retrieved adjacent clauses due to "statutory token blindness", yielding Precision@3 of 84.1%.
- **Hybrid RRF Search** achieved **96.4% Precision@3**, **95.8% Recall@3**, and an **MRR of 0.958**, representing a **12.3% precision improvement** over dense vector search and a **17.9% improvement** over lexical BM25 search.

### B. Microservice Execution Latency Breakdown
Latency was measured across 500 API interactions executed on Groq Cloud Llama 3 infrastructure:

```
+-----------------------------------------+--------------------+
| Pipeline Stage                          | Mean Latency (ms)  |
+-----------------------------------------+--------------------+
| API Gateway Routing & JWT Auth          |        12 ms       |
| Agent 0: Linguistics Translation        |       210 ms       |
| Agent 1: Intent Classification (JSON)   |       185 ms       |
| Agent 2: Hybrid RAG Search (BM25 + RRF) |        95 ms       |
| Agent 3: Risk & Compliance Analysis     |       240 ms       |
| Agent 4: Persona Decision Generation    |       480 ms       |
| Async Audit Telemetry Logging           |         8 ms       |
+-----------------------------------------+--------------------+
| Total End-to-End Execution Latency      |      1230 ms       |
+-----------------------------------------+--------------------+
```
*Table II: Latency Breakdown per Microservice Component.*

The multi-agent pipeline completed in an average of **1.23 seconds**, providing sub-1.5s real-time conversational throughput suitable for high-concurrency production deployments.

### C. Hallucination Suppression & Security Guardrail Verification
To test security guardrails, 50 out-of-domain queries (e.g., general world trivia, programming code generation, external news) were injected into the system.

- **Out-of-Domain Rejection Rate**: UniGuard AI achieved a **100% rejection rate**. When similarity scores fell below threshold $\tau = 0.35$, Agent 2 assigned context to `"None"`, and Agent 4 cleanly rejected the query without generating false information.
- **Prompt Injection Defense**: In 30 adversarial prompt injection tests attempting to override role constraints or bypass university policies, system directives successfully blocked all unauthorized state changes.

### D. Qualitative Discussion & Practical Implications
The empirical results confirm that task decomposition into specialized agents combined with hybrid RRF retrieval solves the primary failure modes of enterprise compliance RAG:
1. **Linguistic Gap Resolution**: Agent 0 successfully bridged Romanized regional inputs and informal slang to formal statutory terms.
2. **Statutory Accuracy**: BM25 preserved exact sub-clause matching, while dense embeddings preserved conceptual search.
3. **Operational Auditing**: The asynchronous SQLite logger ensured non-repudiation without degrading user latency.

## V. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

### A. Conclusion
This paper presented **UniGuard AI**, a microservice-based multi-agent compliance intelligence platform for higher education governance. By coupling a containerized FastAPI architecture with a **Hybrid BM25-Semantic RAG Retrieval Engine** and Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), UniGuard AI effectively resolves both vocabulary mismatch and statutory token blindness. The sequential 5-agent pipeline running on Groq Llama 3 infrastructure provides robust linguistic translation, structured intent classification, automated compliance risk assessment, and persona-tailored decision generation. Empirical evaluation across 250 university policy queries demonstrated **96.4% Precision@3**, **95.8% Recall@3**, **0.958 MRR**, a **100% out-of-domain rejection rate**, and an average latency of **1.23 seconds**.

### B. Future Work
Future extensions will focus on:
1. **Dynamic RRF Weighting**: Implementing adaptive $\alpha$ parameters based on real-time query entity density.
2. **Multimodal Document Ingestion**: Integrating OCR pipelines for historical scanned PDF archives and tabular fee structures.
3. **Automated Human-in-the-Loop Workflow**: Integrating real-time webhooks to trigger administrative review queues automatically when Agent 3 detects high-risk compliance flags (`needs_review: true`).
